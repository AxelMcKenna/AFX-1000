# AUX inhibit candidate — analysis only

No PCB, schematic, or BOM change is authorized by this analysis alone. The parent design task authorizes developing a fix; these calculations identify a candidate and its outstanding acceptance conditions.

The preferred next design screen raises nominal AUX to 3.298633 V by changing P_R108 to 17.6 kΩ (0.1%, 25 ppm/°C), retaining P_R130 and P_R109. Under the existing regulator sensitivity assumptions, the interval is 3.219579–3.378196 V and required input for stated regulation becomes 3.798633 V. `supply_voltage_screen.json` covers all 81 packages / 29 selected part numbers: the limiting recommended upper supply is 3.6 V. This is a supply-pin screen, not approval of functional thresholds or current.

Add TPS3703A5330DSER to E_U31 gate 2's presently AUX-tied pin 6. Retain E_U19, E_U35 and their bypasses for startup qualification. Exact new pin connections and the 87 existing AUX capacitors are in `candidate.json`. This additional supervisor is not pin-compatible with E_U19.

The [TPS3703 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3703.pdf), sections 7.5–7.6, specifies ±0.7% trip accuracy, 0.3–0.8% hysteresis and a 30 µs maximum detection delay across 1.7–5.5 V and −40…125°C, with 5% overdrive, 10 kΩ pull-up and 10 pF output load. CT open gives 7–13 ms release delay for the A variant. Its 300 µs startup delay is nominal only: no maximum startup-correct-state time is claimed. PDF page 7 was rendered and visually checked to distinguish the NOM/MAX columns.

The candidate's lowest 5% overdrive endpoint is 2.957402 V. With **allocated**, not established, 20 mV source/return difference, 100 mA maximum load and 1 µs downstream logic time, retaining the critical rail above 2.4 V needs at least **5.768491 µF effective capacitance**. The static startup and upper-window margins are 37.554 mV and 62.549 mV. A 10 µF effective reservoir would allow 53.740 µs after the overdrive endpoint under that load allocation. Nominal installed capacitance cannot be substituted for effective capacitance. The regulator's 100 mA output rating does not bound actual loads or a rail short.

Before a board edit:

- Audit every AUX-derived threshold and RC timing, the 45 direct resistor roots / 44 networks, all 369 native input contacts and downstream non-AUX receivers. `direct_cross_domain_contacts.json` identifies 11 directly shared contacts, including seven TPS259482 enable inputs, two isolated HOST5 translator outputs, and two clamp LED cathodes. It is **not** a complete series-resistor or connector reachability audit. Translator outputs must be assigned to their actual output supply; AUX VCCA does not mean every output rises with AUX.
- Recalculate source-port currents and resistor power at the proposed maximum; requalify logic thresholds during brownout, nonrail input current, package transitions, driver loading, regulator thermal performance and input dropout. Existing mixed-condition current subtotal does not transfer as an application bound.
- Establish effective reservoir capacitance after bias, temperature, tolerance, aging and assembly; verify worst-source and return paths. Prove the assumed maximum discharge current for the agreed fault model. A hard local AUX short is not covered by finite common-rail bulk capacitance. An isolated reservoir must power the decisive inhibit path as well as the drivers, and its reverse-blocking/dropout/startup behavior needs qualification.
- Verify the specified sense transition/loading or obtain manufacturer confirmation for the actual ramp/fault waveforms. Prove E_U31/E_U29 path delay and low-output validity, particularly below 2.4 V. Test startup ordering; the existing long reset delay cannot be presumed to cover another device's unbounded startup specification.
- Qualify actual MOSFET turn-off separately. The Si8751 35 µs maximum uses 100 pF, not the installed paired CSD18540Q5B load. Its 5 mA minimum Miller-clamp sink is at Vgate=1 V and IMC=50 µA, not an all-voltage gate-discharge bound. A normally closed isolated clamp is a viable architectural alternative to holding the driver alive, but needs full-temperature closure, leakage, peak current, actual gate charge, and startup-interlock bounds. Existing TLP4590AF clamps in the negative startup circuitry provide a reusable topology to examine; no extra clamp has been selected or added.

Alternatives checked in primary sources: TPS3890 and TPS3700 lack maximum assertion delays; TPS3702 also provides nominal timing only. TLV9021 switching timing is typical only. TLV3601 offers a 4.5 ns maximum but at discrete supplies and 50 mV overdrive; a high reference (e.g. REF35205) makes the overdrive arithmetically feasible, but actual common mode/ramp coverage and reference startup remain open. TS3021 tables give maxima at 25°C/discrete supplies. No typical curve was converted into a guaranteed bound.

Run `python -m unittest hardware.qualification.two_board_aux_current_08.fast_inhibit.test_candidate`. Five checks cover infeasible allocations, sensitivity scaling, complete package/capacitor coverage and reproducible artifacts. Self-review found and retained the missing startup maximum, absence of a true current ceiling, discrete comparator timing conditions, and incomplete cross-domain reachability; these remain explicit acceptance items, not hidden defaults.

## Functional follow-up

`functional.py` reuses the existing nodal solver and reference-window corner model against frozen TBP71 connectivity, with proposed values isolated from the board. The unchanged lower-reference window fails the healthy-reference screen by 267.5 mV. Candidate resistor pairs **E_R83/E_R84 = 10.7 kΩ/5.76 kΩ** and **E_R88/E_R89 = 16.2 kΩ/13.3 kΩ**, retaining 0.01%/5 ppm specifications, restore conditional healthy-reference margins of **58.5 mV and 191.5 mV**. These standard nominal values are a candidate, not selected supplier parts. The existing model's ±4 mV comparator offset and ±1.2 nA bias still require operating-point coverage. Passing this screen proves neither the complete faulty-reference exclusion window nor input leakage under every power sequence.

P_U103 senses **VIN_IC**, not a downstream 5 V rail. Raising AUX without correction moves its ideal rising/falling thresholds from **14.40094/14.14146 V** to **18.30741/17.97755 V**. Candidate **P_R118 = 18 kΩ, P_R119 = 11.7 kΩ, P_R117 = 392 kΩ** returns them to **14.39651/14.13649 V**. Keeping C109 at 1 nF increases its reference filter from 5.000 to 7.091 µs; a **680 pF C0G candidate** gives 4.822 µs. This affects noise rejection and fault response. Comparator internal hysteresis, offset, output swing, actual RC tolerance and slew remain to be qualified; nominal trip restoration is not a worst-case timing proof.

Re-solving all 44 disjoint direct-AUX resistor networks (45 roots / 77 resistors) at the proposed rail endpoints gives **19.246026 mA**, up from **15.474894 mA** under the old voltage screen. This retains the prior unaccepted ±3% total resistor envelope, ideal sinks/switch and zero pin leakage. It excludes package currents and separately owned output branches. Per-resistor worst-corner power is recorded in `functional.json`; dissipation ratings and mounted derating are not inferred from resistor values.

All **87 capacitor voltage ratings** are above 3.378196 V; the lowest marked rating is 16 V. This does not establish their effective capacitance. N_U56's independent supply is **NF_RAW5**, and its reset controls N_U57's switched clamp-LED rail. N_U58 reports ABS_AUX_FAULT. N_U56 remains a TPS3890 with no maximum falling delay, so independence of its supply does not supply a missing timing guarantee.

Startup capacitor-charge sensitivities use the actual E_U19 C60 (10 nF, initial ±5%) and N_U56 C99 (100 nF, allocated ±6%), together with manufacturer CT threshold/current extremes. These are **8.233–15.050 ms** and **81.467–151.933 ms**, before base delay and with zero board/CT leakage. They are not full startup-delay bounds: initial device startup and zero-capacitance base propagation are nominal-only. Increasing AUX advances when the old AUX-sense thresholds are crossed relative to final rail settling. New TPS3703 qualification and existing reference-valid logic must both participate in release.

At **13.2 V input, 85°C ambient and an allocated 100 mA load**, the proposed regulator dissipates about **0.999 W** and requires installed θJA ≤ **40.03°C/W** to keep junction ≤125°C. The datasheet's 66.2°C/W DGN test-board value would give about **151.2°C**; it is not a measurement of this PCB. At 65 mA the corresponding requirement is ≤61.53°C/W. The existing component temperature screen does not establish that 85°C ambient is a product requirement. The design needs a declared enclosure ambient/load envelope and verified thermal path; changing AUX voltage alone does not qualify heating.

The functional tests catch the uncorrected startup failure, restore both nominal input-OV transitions, retain complete resistor/cap inventories and reject interpreting thermal sensitivities as acceptance. Together with previous checks, **23 tests pass**. Self-review also corrected `operating_bounds/README.md`: the retained startup-cell pads and listed proximity layers are **B.Cu**, not F.Cu. The JSON proximity calculation already used the native same-surface layers; no routing geometry changed.
