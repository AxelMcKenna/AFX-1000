# Original PCB mount stack audit

The retained prototype BOM specifies M2×10 pan-head screws, 0.5 mm washers, nominal AF4.0×1.6 mm nuts and 6 mm feet. Its actual hex-pocket roof is Z1.8 (Z−0.1 plus 1.9 mm cut). The PCB support tops are Z6.75 for signal and Z7.9 for power; board thickness is 1.6 mm.

This puts nominal screw tips at Z−1.15 for signal and Z0 for power. Those below-floor tips are intentional and clear the nominal table plane at Z−6. The split-floor screws reach Z−5.2 and already control the stated 0.8 mm nominal table gap. Below-floor protrusion alone does not justify deepening a nut pocket or rejecting a longer support fastener. Actual feet, placement and compression remain unqualified.

The original 24 mounts comprise 11 signal and 13 power positions. Current generic head geometry covers 22: D_H2 and D_H5 belong to the sensor assembly and must not be overwritten by a generic hardware revision. Their original stack appears in the audit only for provenance.

Nominal tip protrusion past the 1.6 mm nut is 1.35 mm for signal and 0.2 mm for power. An illustrative 0.4 mm incomplete-tip allocation leaves 0.95 mm and −0.2 mm respectively. This is a screening assumption borrowed from the explicit touch acceptance budget, not a manufacturer limit or proof that the original power screw cannot carry its load. Exact screw tip/thread geometry, length tolerances, washer specification and physical retention require qualification. An M2×8 at the original power stack ends at Z2.0, above the Z1.8 nut roof, so nominal engagement is absent with the stated washer.

No geometry or hardware selection changes here. The next hardware revision should use sourced parts and complete stack tolerances, preserve sensor-owned mounts, include shaft/nut/washer geometry and check feet/service clearance. Current generic head reservations alone do not prove that full stack. The historical prototype package remains frozen.
